2.1 — Deduce Difficult Word Meanings from Context

What is contextual deduction?

Contextual deduction means inferring the meaning of an unfamiliar word from the words, phrases, sentences and situation surrounding it.

Roman Urdu trick: Unknown word ko dekh kar panic nahi karna. Sentence ke clues ko detective ki tarah connect karein: definition, example, contrast, cause, result aur overall mood.

Types of contextual clues

ClueHow it worksExample
DefinitionThe writer directly explains the word.A herbivore, an animal that eats plants, avoids meat.
SynonymA similar word appears nearby.The route was arduous, extremely difficult and tiring.
Antonym/contrastOpposite information gives the meaning.Unlike his generous sister, he was parsimonious.
ExampleSpecific examples identify the category.Arboreal animals, such as monkeys and sloths, live in trees.
Cause/effectA cause or result clarifies the word.The ground was parched; months without rain had dried it.
InferenceThe situation suggests the meaning indirectly.After the long march, the soldiers were debilitated.

Context deduction procedure

  1. Read the entire sentence, not just the unknown word.
  2. Read one sentence before and after it.
  3. Identify the part of speech.
  4. Look for signal words: that is, meaning, unlike, but, however, such as, therefore, because, consequently.
  5. Predict a rough meaning before looking at the options.
  6. Replace the word with the predicted meaning.
  7. Check grammar, logic, tone and precision.

Signal words

Denotation, connotation and precision

Denotation is the basic dictionary meaning. Connotation is the feeling or association a word carries.

Memory trick: Dictionary meaning = denotation. Emotional colour = connotation. Same family ka word ho sakta hai, lekin tone bilkul change ho sakti hai.

2.2 — Synonyms, Shades of Meaning, Irony, Parody and Satire

Synonyms are not always interchangeable

Synonyms share a similar core meaning, but they may differ in intensity, formality, emotional tone, typical usage or implied judgment.

A formal synonym is not automatically better. The correct word must fit the audience, purpose and sentence.

Irony

Irony involves a meaningful contrast between appearance and reality, expectation and outcome, or literal wording and intended meaning.

  • Verbal irony: Words suggest a meaning different from the literal wording, often through tone.
  • Situational irony: The outcome sharply contrasts with what is expected.
  • Dramatic irony: The audience knows something important that a character does not know.
A student arrives three hours late and says, “I am exceptionally punctual today.” The statement is verbally ironic.

Parody

Parody imitates the style, structure or features of a recognizable work, person or genre, usually for humour or criticism.

A comedy imitates the exaggerated language of a serious heroic speech to make the style look ridiculous.

Satire

Satire uses humour, irony, exaggeration or ridicule to expose foolishness, corruption, hypocrisy or social problems.

A fictional city creates a “Department of Common Sense” that never makes a sensible decision. The target is bureaucratic incompetence.
FeatureIronyParodySatire
Main focusContrastImitationCriticism of a flaw

PurposeCreate contrast or implied meaningExpose stylistic habits or entertainEncourage reflection or criticize society

2.3 — Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement

Basic rule

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent, the noun or noun phrase to which it refers, in number and appropriate person. In some contexts, gender or natural gender also matters.

Incorrect: The students submitted his assignment.
Correct: The students submitted their assignments.

Number agreement

Singular antecedentPlural antecedent
The boy lost his pen.The boys lost their pens.
The machine completed its task.The machines completed their tasks.

Indefinite pronouns

Always singularAlways pluralDepends on noun after “of”
each, everyone, everybody, anyone, anybody, someone, nobody, neither, eitherboth, few, many, severalall, some, none, most, any
Everyone must bring his or her/their ID.Many have completed their work.Some of the water has lost its purity.
Some of the students have submitted their work.

In modern standard English, singular they/their is widely accepted for an individual whose gender is unknown or irrelevant. Follow the style expected by the exam or institution.

Compound antecedents

  • Noun joined by and is usually plural: “Ali and Hamza brought their books.”
  • Nouns joined by or/nor: agreement commonly follows the nearer antecedent in formal constructions, though rewriting is often clearer.
  • Collective nouns depend on whether the group acts as one unit or as individuals, and on the variety of English being used.

Ambiguous reference

Unclear: When Ahmed spoke to Bilal, he was angry.
Clear: Ahmed was angry when he spoke to Bilal.
Clear: When Ahmed spoke to Bilal, Bilal was angry.
Roman Urdu: Pronoun ka “address” clear hona chahiye. Agar sentence mein do male names hon aur “he” samajh na aaye kis ko refer kar raha hai, sentence ambiguous hai.

Common traps

  • Words between antecedent and pronoun do not change the antecedent’s number.
  • Each and every are grammatically singular.
  • A phrase beginning with of may distract you from the real antecedent.
  • Do not confuse subject agreement with pronoun agreement.

2.4 — Tenses

English traditionally teaches 12 tense-aspect forms: present, past and future combined with simple, continuous, perfect and perfect continuous.

TimeSimpleContinuousPerfectPerfect continuous
Presentwrite/writesam/is/are writinghas/have writtenhas/have been writing
Pastwrotewas/were writinghad writtenhad been writing
Futurewill writewill be writingwill have writtenwill have been writing

Present tenses

TenseFormMain useExample
Present simpleSubject + base verb/sHabit, fact, routine, timetableWater boils at 100°C.
Present continuousam/is/are + -ingAction now, temporary situation, arranged futureShe is preparing for the test.
Present perfecthas/have + past participlePast action linked to present; life experience; unfinished timeHe has completed three chapters.
Present perfect continuoushas/have been + -ingDuration continuing to now or recent visible resultThey have been studying for two hours.

Past tenses

TenseFormMain useExample
Past simplePast formCompleted past eventShe visited Lahore last week.
Past continuouswas/were + -ingAction in progress at a past time; background actionI was reading when he called.
Past perfecthad + past participleEarlier of two past eventsThe train had left before we arrived.
Past perfect continuoushad been + -ingDuration before a past pointShe had been waiting for an hour before the bus came.

Future tenses

TenseFormMain useExample
Future simplewill + base verbPrediction, decision, promiseI will help you.
Future continuouswill be + -ingAction in progress at a future timeAt 8 p.m., I will be revising.
Future perfectwill have + past participleCompleted before a future deadlineBy Friday, she will have finished the unit.
Future perfect continuouswill have been + -ingDuration up to a future pointBy June, he will have been working here for a year.

High-yield tense rules

  • After did, use the base verb: “Did he go?” not “Did he went?”
  • After has/have/had in perfect tenses, use the past participle: “has eaten,” “had written.”
  • Use present simple in many time clauses referring to the future: “When he arrives, we will begin.”
  • Stative verbs such as know, believe, own, understand are not normally used in continuous forms in ordinary usage.
  • Use since for a starting point and for for a duration: “since Monday,” “for three days.”
  • Use past perfect only when the sequence of past events needs clarification or emphasis.
Roman Urdu formula: Simple = routine/fact. Continuous = action chal raha hai. Perfect = action complete with a connection. Perfect continuous = duration par focus.

2.5 — Infinitives and Infinitive Phrases

What is an infinitive?

An infinitive is usually to + base verb, such as to read, to understand, to succeed. A bare infinitive is the base verb without to.

Functions of infinitives

FunctionExampleRole
Noun-like subjectTo learn is valuable.Subject
ObjectShe wants to improve.Object of wants
ComplementHis aim is to qualify.Subject complement
Adjective-likeI have a book to read.Modifies book
Adverb-like purposeHe studied hard to pass.Explains purpose

Infinitive phrases

An infinitive phrase contains an infinitive plus its objects, complements or modifiers.

To solve the difficult problem without assistance requires patience.
Infinitive: to solve
Object: the difficult problem
Modifier: without assistance
Whole phrase: subject of the sentence.

To-infinitive vs bare infinitive

Use to-infinitiveUse bare infinitive
want to golet him go
decide to leavemake her leave
hope to succeedmodal + base verb: can go, must study
It is easy to understand.had better revise

Advanced forms

FormExampleMeaning
Passive infinitiveto be examinedAction received by subject
Perfect infinitiveto have finishedEarlier action
Perfect passive infinitiveto have been selectedEarlier received action
Continuous infinitiveto be studyingAction in progress

Common infinitive errors

  • After a modal, do not add to: “She can swim,” not “can to swim.”
  • After make and let in active constructions, use the bare infinitive.
  • After allow, use an object + to-infinitive: “They allowed him to leave.”
  • Do not confuse an infinitive phrase with a prepositional phrase beginning with to.
  • A split infinitive is not automatically incorrect: “to carefully examine” is often clear and natural.
Roman Urdu trick: Infinitive ko aksar “to + verb” se pehchanein, lekin modal, let aur make ke baad bare verb aata hai. “To” har jagah infinitive ka signal nahi hota.

2.6 — Gerunds and Gerund Phrases

What is a gerund?

A gerund is the -ing form of a verb functioning as a noun.

Swimming improves stamina.
“Swimming” comes from a verb but functions as the subject, so it is a gerund.

Gerund functions

FunctionExample
SubjectReading improves vocabulary.
Object of a verbShe enjoys reading.
Object of a prepositionHe is interested in learning.
Subject complementHer favourite activity is painting.
After possessiveI appreciate your helping me.

Gerund phrases

A gerund phrase includes a gerund plus its object, complement or modifiers.

Carefully reviewing the evidence before the hearing prevented a serious mistake.
Gerund: reviewing
Object: the evidence
Modifiers: carefully and before the hearing
Whole phrase: subject.

Gerund vs present participle

GerundPresent participle
Functions as a noun: Running is healthy.Functions in a verb phrase or as an adjective: She is running.
His hobby is painting.The painting child smiled.

Verbs followed by gerunds

Common examples: enjoy, avoid, admit, deny, consider, suggest, finish, postpone, practise, risk, mind.

Correct: She avoided making the same mistake.
Incorrect in standard exam usage: She avoided to make the same mistake.

Gerund or infinitive: meaning can change

ExpressionMeaning
remember doingRecall a past action
remember to doNot forget a future/intended action
stop doingQuit an activity
stop to doPause one activity in order to do another
try doingExperiment with a method
try to doMake an effort
Roman Urdu trick: Gerund -ing form hota hai jo noun ka kaam karta hai. Agar “-ing” subject, object ya preposition ke baad noun ki position mein hai, gerund ka chance strong hai.

Advanced Practice MCQs

Attempt the questions, then reveal the answer and explanation.

0 of 18 answered correctly

1. “The scientist’s explanation was plausible but not conclusive.” Plausible means:

C. “But not conclusive” indicates that it is believable without being final proof.

2. Which word has the strongest negative shade?

B. “Miserly” strongly suggests an excessively unwilling attitude toward spending.

3. A comedy imitates the style of a famous epic poem to mock its exaggerated heroism. This is primarily:

D. It imitates a recognizable style for comic or critical effect.

4. A fictional office creates a committee to eliminate unnecessary committees, then forms five new committees. This most clearly illustrates:

A. The situation criticizes bureaucratic inefficiency through exaggeration and humour.

5. Choose the correct sentence.

C. “Each” is singular in traditional formal agreement. Singular “they” is also widely accepted in contemporary English.

6. “Some of the water has evaporated, but some of the students have remained.” Why do the verbs differ?

B. “Water” is uncountable singular; “students” is plural.

7. “When Ahmed met Bilal, he was upset.” The main grammatical problem is:

D. “He” may refer to Ahmed or Bilal. Rewrite for clarity.

8. Which sentence correctly uses the present perfect?

A. Present perfect = has/have + past participle.

9. “By next Monday, they ______ the project.”

C. Future perfect describes completion before a future deadline.

10. “I was studying when the lights went out.” The past continuous expresses:

B. “Was studying” is the background action; “went out” is the interrupting event.

11. Choose the correct form: “She decided ______ harder.”

D. “Decide” is normally followed by a to-infinitive.

12. Which sentence contains an infinitive phrase functioning as the subject?

A. “To understand the theory” is the subject of “requires.”

13. Which sentence correctly uses a bare infinitive?

C. Modal verbs are followed by the base form without “to.”

14. In “He avoided answering the question,” “answering” is:

B. “Avoid” is followed by a gerund phrase: “answering the question.”

15. Which sentence contains a gerund phrase as the subject?

D. “Reviewing the evidence carefully” functions as the subject.

16. “He stopped smoking” means:

A. Stop + gerund means cease the activity.

17. “He stopped to check the map” means:

C. Stop + to-infinitive expresses purpose.

18. Which sentence contains a perfect infinitive?

B. “To have forgotten” is the perfect infinitive: to + have + past participle.

Final One-Page Revision

  • Context clues: definition, synonym, antonym, example, cause-effect and inference.
  • Synonyms may differ in intensity, register, connotation and emotional tone.
  • Irony = contrast; parody = imitation; satire = criticism through humour or ridicule.
  • Pronouns must agree with antecedents in number and appropriate person.
  • Each/every/everyone are traditionally singular; all/some/most depend on the noun after “of.”
  • Simple = fact/routine; continuous = action in progress; perfect = completed/link; perfect continuous = duration.
  • Infinitive = to + base verb, but modals, let and make commonly take the bare infinitive.
  • Gerund = -ing form functioning as a noun; gerund phrase includes its modifiers and objects.
  • Stop doing = quit; stop to do = pause for a purpose.
Master formula: Meaning ko context se verify karein → synonym ka shade check karein → pronoun ka antecedent identify karein → tense ka time relationship samjhein → infinitive/gerund ka grammatical function dekhein.
