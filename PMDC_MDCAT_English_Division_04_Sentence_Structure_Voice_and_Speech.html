2.11 — Sentence Classes, Phrases and Meaning

Sentence, clause and phrase

UnitDefinitionExample
SentenceA complete grammatical unit expressing a complete thought.The students revised grammar.
ClauseA group of words containing a subject and a finite verb.because the students revised
PhraseA group of related words functioning as a unit without a complete subject–finite-verb structure.after the lecture

Four sentence classes by structure

Simple

One independent clause.

The candidate revised.

Compound

Two or more independent clauses joined appropriately.

She revised, and she practised.

Complex

One independent clause plus at least one dependent clause.

She practised because the test was near.

Compound-complex

At least two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause.

She revised, and he practised because the test was near.

Four sentence functions

TypePurposeExample
DeclarativeStatement or assertionThe result is reliable.
InterrogativeQuestionIs the result reliable?
ImperativeCommand, instruction, request or adviceCheck the result.
ExclamatoryStrong emotion or reactionWhat a reliable result!

Major phrase types

PhraseFunctionExample
Noun phraseActs as a noun; can be subject, object or complement.The exceptionally careful student passed.
Verb phraseMain verb plus auxiliaries.She has been studying.
Adjective phraseModifies a noun/pronoun.The answer full of errors was rejected.
Adverb phraseModifies a verb, adjective, adverb or clause.He solved it with great care.
Prepositional phraseBegins with a preposition and ends with its object.The book on the table is mine.
Gerund phrase-ing form functioning as a noun, with dependents.Solving difficult questions requires practice.
Infinitive phraseTo + base verb with dependents.She came to revise the chapter.
Participial phrasePresent or past participle functioning adjectivally.Exhausted by the test, he rested.

Phrase placement changes meaning

Unclear: I saw a man with binoculars.
Possible meaning 1: I used binoculars to see a man.
Possible meaning 2: I saw a man who had binoculars.
Clearer: With binoculars, I saw a man.
Or: I saw a man who was carrying binoculars.
Misplaced modifier: Walking through the corridor, the examination hall appeared empty.
Problem: The hall appears to be walking.
Correct: Walking through the corridor, I noticed that the examination hall was empty.

Restrictive vs non-restrictive phrases/clauses

RestrictiveNon-restrictive
Essential to identify the noun; no commas.Extra information; usually set off by commas.
The students who studied passed.My brother, who studied all night, passed.
Roman Urdu trick: Phrase ko sentence ka “role-player” samjhein. Dekhein woh noun ka kaam kar raha hai, noun ko describe kar raha hai, ya verb ko modify kar raha hai. Position badlegi to “kis ko describe kar raha hai?” ka jawab bhi badal sakta hai.

2.12 — Sentence Inversion

Inversion means changing the usual word order, commonly placing an auxiliary or another element before the subject. Normal order is usually Subject → Verb → Object; inversion deliberately changes this order.

Types and purposes

TypePatternExample
Question inversionAuxiliary + subject + verbAre you ready?
Negative-frontingNegative expression + auxiliary + subjectNever have I seen such a result.
Conditional inversionHad/Were/Should + subjectHad I known, I would have prepared.
So/neither inversionSo/neither + auxiliary + subjectSo did I. Neither can he.
Locative inversionPlace expression + verb + subjectOn the wall hangs a portrait.
Emphatic inversionFronted element + auxiliary + subjectOnly then did she understand.

Negative and restrictive expressions that trigger inversion

When these expressions begin a formal clause, inversion is often required:

Never
Never have I forgotten the lesson.
Rarely/Seldom
Rarely does he make such errors.
Hardly/Scarcely
Hardly had we arrived when it started raining.
Not only
Not only did she pass, but she also excelled.
Only then
Only then did the truth emerge.
Under no circumstances
Under no circumstances should you ignore the instructions.

Conditional inversion

Ordinary conditionalInverted conditional
If I had known, I would have acted.Had I known, I would have acted.
If she were ready, she would begin.Were she ready, she would begin.
If you should need help, call me.Should you need help, call me.
Roman Urdu trick: “Negative word front mein aya, helping verb ko subject se pehle bhejo.” Never + have + I; Rarely + does + he; Only then + did + she.

Common inversion errors

Incorrect: Never I have seen this.
Correct: Never have I seen this.
Incorrect: Only then she understood.
Correct: Only then did she understand.
Incorrect: Not only he failed, but he also complained.
Correct: Not only did he fail, but he also complained.
Important: Do not invert after every fronted adverb. “Yesterday, I studied” is normal. Inversion is triggered by particular negative, restrictive, conditional or stylistic structures.

2.13 — Active and Passive Voice

What is voice?

Voice shows whether the grammatical subject performs an action or receives it.

Active

The subject performs the action.

The researcher analysed the data.

Passive

The subject receives the action.

The data were analysed by the researcher.

Basic transformation

Active: Subject + verb + object
Passive: Object + appropriate form of be + past participle + (by + agent)

Only transitive verbs normally form a standard passive because they take a direct object.

Tense conversion table

TenseActivePassive
Present simpleThey write letters.Letters are written.
Present continuousThey are writing letters.Letters are being written.
Present perfectThey have written letters.Letters have been written.
Past simpleThey wrote letters.Letters were written.
Past continuousThey were writing letters.Letters were being written.
Past perfectThey had written letters.Letters had been written.
Future simpleThey will write letters.Letters will be written.
Future perfectThey will have written letters.Letters will have been written.
ModalThey can write letters.Letters can be written.

Questions, commands and special patterns

ActivePassive
Did she complete the task?Was the task completed by her?
Who broke the window?By whom was the window broken?
Open the door.Let the door be opened.
People believe that he is honest.It is believed that he is honest. / He is believed to be honest.
Someone has stolen my bag.My bag has been stolen.

When passive voice is appropriate

  • When the receiver of the action is more important than the agent: “The patient was examined.”
  • When the agent is unknown: “The window was broken.”
  • When the agent is obvious or irrelevant: “The form was submitted.”
  • In formal scientific and procedural writing: “The sample was heated to 80°C.”
  • To maintain topic continuity: “The solution was filtered. It was then tested.”

When active voice is usually better

Use active voice when clarity, responsibility and directness matter: “The laboratory assistant recorded the temperature” is clearer than “The temperature was recorded,” if the assistant's identity is relevant.

Roman Urdu trick: Passive mein object ko front par lao, “be” ki tense wali form lagao, phir verb ki third form. Active ka object hi passive ka subject ban jata hai. Agent zaroori na ho to “by” phrase hata sakte hain.

Common passive errors

Incorrect: The letter was wrote by Ali.
Correct: The letter was written by Ali.
Incorrect: The problem was solved by him yesterday carefully.
Better: The problem was solved carefully by him yesterday.
Incorrect: The accident was happened yesterday.
Correct: The accident happened yesterday.
“Happen” is intransitive and normally does not take a standard passive.

2.14 — Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct vs indirect speech

Direct speech

Reports the speaker's exact words within quotation marks.

Ali said, “I am tired.”

Indirect speech

Reports the meaning without quoting the exact words.

Ali said that he was tired.

Main changes in reported speech

DirectCommon reported form
Present simplePast simple
Present continuousPast continuous
Present perfectPast perfect
Past simplePast perfect, where backshift is required
WillWould
CanCould
MayMight
MustHad to, when expressing past obligation

Pronoun and time/place changes

DirectReported possibility
Ihe/she, depending on speaker
thisthat
thesethose
nowthen
todaythat day
yesterdaythe previous day
tomorrowthe next/following day
herethere
last weekthe previous week

Statements

Direct: She said, “I have completed the work.”
Indirect: She said that she had completed the work.

“That” is often optional after common reporting verbs such as said and thought.

Yes/no questions

Direct: He asked, “Are you ready?”
Indirect: He asked if/whether I was ready.

Reported questions use statement word order. Do not retain question inversion.

Wh-questions

Direct: She asked, “Where do you live?”
Indirect: She asked where I lived.
Incorrect: She asked where did I live.
Correct: She asked where I lived.

Commands, requests and advice

DirectIndirect
He said, “Open the door.”He ordered me to open the door.
She said, “Please help me.”She requested me to help her.
He said, “Do not touch it.”He warned me not to touch it.
The teacher said, “Revise this topic.”The teacher advised us to revise that topic.

Exclamations and wishes

Direct: She said, “What a beautiful view!”
Indirect: She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful view.
Direct: He said, “May you succeed!”
Indirect: He wished/prayed that I might succeed.

When backshift may not be necessary

  • If the reporting verb is present: “She says, ‘I am tired’” → She says that she is tired.
  • If the statement expresses a universal truth: “The teacher said, ‘The Earth revolves around the Sun’” → The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.
  • If the fact remains true and the context permits, speakers may retain the original tense.
Roman Urdu trick: Reported speech mein teen cheezen check karein: speaker ka pronoun, tense ka backshift, aur time/place words. Question ko indirect banate waqt “question order” nahi, statement order use hota hai.

Advanced Practice MCQs

Attempt each question, then reveal the answer and explanation.

0 of 20 answered correctly

1. Which sentence contains a noun phrase functioning as the subject?

C. “The exceptionally diligent candidate” is a noun phrase functioning as subject.

2. Identify the sentence type: “Although the test was difficult, the candidate remained calm.”

A. It has one independent clause and one dependent concessive clause.

3. In “The book on the top shelf belongs to me,” what does “on the top shelf” modify?

D. The prepositional phrase identifies which book.

4. Choose the sentence with a correctly placed modifier.

B. The introductory participial phrase logically modifies “I.”

5. Which sentence is compound-complex?

C. It contains two independent clauses and one dependent clause.

6. Choose the correct inverted form of “I have never seen such precision.”

A. “Never” at the front triggers auxiliary–subject inversion.

7. Choose the correct sentence.

D. Use “did + subject + base verb.”

8. Which is the correct inverted conditional form of “If I had known, I would have helped”?

B. Past perfect conditional inversion uses “Had + subject + past participle.”

9. Convert to passive: “The technician is repairing the machine.”

C. Present continuous passive is “is/are being + past participle.”

10. Convert to passive: “They had completed the experiment.”

A. Past perfect passive is “had been + past participle.”

11. Which sentence is most appropriate when the agent is unknown?

D. Passive voice allows the unknown agent to remain unmentioned.

12. Which verb normally cannot form a standard passive?

B. “Happen” is normally intransitive and does not take a direct object.

13. Report: Ali said, “I am preparing for the test.”

C. “I” changes to “he,” and present continuous commonly backshifts to past continuous.

14. Report: She asked, “Do you understand the rule?”

A. Yes/no questions use if/whether and statement word order.

15. Report: He asked, “Where did you put the file?”

D. Reported wh-questions use statement order and commonly backshift past simple to past perfect.

16. Report: The teacher said, “Do not ignore the instructions.”

B. Negative commands commonly become “warn/tell + object + not to + verb.”

17. Which reported statement correctly preserves a universal truth?

C. A universal truth can remain in the present tense.

18. Which sentence uses passive voice to foreground the receiver and omit an irrelevant agent?

A. The application is the discourse focus, and the agent is omitted.

19. Which sentence correctly uses negative-fronting?

D. After “does,” use the base form “make.”

20. Which sentence has the clearest communicative focus?

B. Active voice directly identifies the responsible agent.

Final One-Page Revision

  • Identify whether a group of words is a phrase, dependent clause or independent clause.
  • Sentence structure: simple, compound, complex and compound-complex.
  • Sentence function: declarative, interrogative, imperative and exclamatory.
  • Modifier placement determines which noun or verb is being described.
  • Negative or restrictive fronting often triggers auxiliary–subject inversion.
  • Conditional inversion uses Had, Were or Should without “if.”
  • Passive = appropriate form of “be” + past participle; the active object becomes the passive subject.
  • Passive voice foregrounds the receiver, hides an unknown agent or suits formal process writing.
  • Reported questions use statement word order, not question inversion.
  • For indirect speech, check pronouns, tense backshift, time/place words and reporting verb.
Master formula: Structure identify karein → phrase ka target find karein → inversion trigger check karein → voice ka focus decide karein → direct speech ko report karte waqt pronoun, tense aur word order verify karein.
