3.1 — Proofreading and Editing

Proofreading vs editing

Editing

Improves meaning, structure, logic, tone, clarity, word choice and organization.

It asks: Is the writing effective?

Proofreading

Checks the nearly final text for grammar, spelling, punctuation, spacing, capitalization and formatting errors.

It asks: Is the final text correct?

A practical editing sequence

  1. Content: Is the claim accurate, relevant and complete?
  2. Organization: Does every sentence support the paragraph's main idea?
  3. Sentence structure: Are clauses joined correctly and modifiers placed logically?
  4. Usage: Are tense, agreement, pronouns, prepositions and word functions correct?
  5. Style: Is the wording clear, concise, consistent and suitable for the audience?
  6. Mechanics: Check spelling, punctuation, capitalization and formatting.

Common editing targets

ProblemWeak versionImproved version
WordinessDue to the fact that he was late, he missed it.Because he was late, he missed it.
Vague pronounWhen Sara spoke to Ayesha, she was upset.Sara was upset when she spoke to Ayesha.
RedundancyReturn back the book.Return the book.
Informal styleThe results were kind of surprising.The results were somewhat surprising.
Unnecessary passiveIt was decided by the committee that the rule should be changed.The committee decided to change the rule.
Inconsistent tenseHe enters the room and sat down.He entered the room and sat down.
Unclear comparisonAli's marks are higher than Ahmed.Ali's marks are higher than Ahmed's.

Usage and style checklist

Clarity
Prefer precise nouns and active verbs.
Concision
Remove words that add no meaning.
Consistency
Keep tense, spelling variety and formatting consistent.
Parallelism
Use matching grammatical forms in a series.
Appropriate tone
Formal writing should avoid slang and vague fillers.
Logical flow
Use transitions that accurately show relationships.
Editing example: “The researcher was careful, accurate, and recorded the data systematically.”
Error: The series is not parallel: adjective, adjective, verb phrase.
Correction 1: “The researcher was careful, accurate, and systematic.”
Correction 2: “The researcher worked carefully, recorded data accurately, and maintained systematic records.”
Roman Urdu trick: Editing ko 3 rounds mein karein: pehle meaning aur structure, phir grammar aur usage, aur akhir mein spelling/punctuation. Har cheez aik hi waqt mein check karna dimagh ko unnecessarily overload karta hai.

3.2 — Faulty Sentence Structure

1. Sentence fragment

A fragment is an incomplete sentence presented as if it were complete. It may lack a subject, a finite verb or an independent clause.

Fragment: Because the examination was difficult.
Correct: Because the examination was difficult, many candidates needed more time.
Fragment: Running through the corridor.
Correct: The student was running through the corridor.

2. Run-on sentence

A run-on joins two independent clauses without suitable punctuation or a conjunction.

Incorrect: The lecture ended we started the test.
Correct: The lecture ended, and we started the test.
Correct: The lecture ended; we started the test.
Correct: When the lecture ended, we started the test.

3. Comma splice

A comma splice joins two independent clauses with only a comma.

Incorrect: The data were incomplete, the conclusion was unreliable.
Correct: The data were incomplete, so the conclusion was unreliable.
Correct: The data were incomplete; the conclusion was unreliable.

4. Misplaced and dangling modifiers

Error typeProblemCorrection
Misplaced modifierThe phrase is too far from the word it modifies.She almost failed every test. / She failed almost every test. These have different meanings.
Dangling modifierThe implied doer of the introductory phrase is missing or illogical.After studying all night, I felt exhausted.
Dangling: After reading the report, the conclusion seemed weak.
Correct: After reading the report, I found the conclusion weak.

5. Faulty parallelism

Items joined by and, or, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also and similar structures should normally have matching grammatical forms.

Incorrect: The course aims to develop reading, writing and how students think critically.
Correct: The course aims to develop reading, writing and critical thinking.
Incorrect: She likes to analyse data, writing reports and presentations.
Correct: She likes analysing data, writing reports and preparing presentations.

6. Faulty coordination and subordination

RelationshipUseful structureExample
Additionand, moreover, furthermoreThe method is simple, and it is reliable.
Contrastbut, although, whereasAlthough the method is simple, it is reliable.
Causebecause, since, asThe result changed because the sample was contaminated.
Effectso, therefore, consequentlyThe sample was contaminated; therefore, the result changed.
Conditionif, unless, provided thatUnless the sample is clean, the result may be unreliable.

7. Ambiguous structure

Ambiguous: The teacher told the student that he had made an error.
Who made the error? The teacher or the student?
Clearer: The teacher told the student, “You have made an error.”
Or: The teacher, who had made an error, informed the student.
Roman Urdu trick: Har sentence ka “skeleton” dekhein: subject + finite verb. Agar do complete thoughts hain to unke darmiyan full stop, semicolon, ya coordinating conjunction chahiye. Sirf comma aksar kaafi nahi hota.

3.3 — Subject–Verb Agreement

Subject–verb agreement means that the verb must agree with its subject in number and person. The main trap is that words between the subject and verb can distract you.

Core rules

RuleExample
Singular subject → singular verbThe student works hard.
Plural subject → plural verbThe students work hard.
Ignore intervening phrasesThe quality of the answers is important.
And usually makes a plural subjectAli and Sara are ready.
Each/every + singular noun → singular verbEvery candidate has an identity card.
Either/neither + singular alternatives → singular verbNeither answer is correct.
There is/are agrees with the real subjectThere are several errors.
Titles and names of works often take singular verbsGreat Expectations is a novel.

High-frequency traps

ConstructionCorrect agreementReason
A number of + plural nounA number of students are absent.“A number of” means several.
The number of + plural nounThe number of students is increasing.The subject is “number.”
Along with / as well as / together withThe teacher, along with two assistants, is present.The added phrase does not change the main subject.
Collective nounThe committee has reached its decision.As one unit, it is singular in standard formal usage.
Fractions/percentagesHalf of the water is clean. Half of the samples are clean.Agreement depends on the noun after “of.”
Relative pronounShe is one of the students who work hard.“Who” refers to students.
One of + singular relative clauseShe is the only one of the students who works hard.“Who” refers to the only one.

Indefinite pronouns

Always singular
each, everyone, everybody, anyone, anybody, someone, somebody, nobody, neither, either
Everyone is ready.
Always plural
both, few, many, several
Several were absent.
Depends on context
all, some, most, none, any
Some of the water is gone.
Some of the books are missing.

Special constructions

More than one: More than one student has complained. Although the meaning is plural, the construction is normally singular.
Many a: Many a candidate has made this error. “Many a” takes a singular noun and singular verb.
Neither...nor: Neither the teacher nor the students are ready. The verb commonly agrees with the nearer subject, “students.”
Either...or: Either the assistants or the manager is responsible.
Roman Urdu trick: Verb se pehle sab se pehle main subject pakrein. “of the answers,” “along with the teachers,” ya “who were absent” jaisi phrases ko temporarily side par rakhein. Phir verb choose karein.

3.4 — Errors of Functions and Spellings

Word functions

FunctionRoleExample
NounNames a person, place, thing, idea or quality.Accuracy matters.
PronounReplaces a noun phrase.They revised.
VerbExpresses action, event or state.She analysed the data.
AdjectiveModifies a noun or pronoun.a careful student
AdverbModifies a verb, adjective, adverb or clause.She worked carefully.
PrepositionShows relation of a noun/pronoun to another word.on the table
ConjunctionJoins words, phrases or clauses.and, but, because
InterjectionExpresses sudden emotion.Oh! That is surprising.

Common function errors

IncorrectCorrectWhy
He did good in the test.He did well in the test.“Well” is the adverb modifying “did.”
She speaks English fluent.She speaks English fluently.An adverb modifies “speaks.”
It was a successfully experiment.It was a successful experiment.An adjective modifies “experiment.”
He is real tired.He is really tired.“Really” modifies the adjective “tired.”
Between you and IBetween you and meObject of the preposition requires an object pronoun.
Me and Ali went there.Ali and I went there.Use subject pronouns in subject position.

Confusing word pairs

PairMeaning and example
affect / effectAffect is usually a verb: Stress affects memory. Effect is usually a noun: The effect was significant.
accept / exceptAccept = receive or agree. Except = excluding.
advice / adviseAdvice = noun. Advise = verb.
principle / principalPrinciple = rule or belief. Principal = main or head of an institution.
compliment / complementCompliment = praise. Complement = completes or matches.
stationary / stationeryStationary = not moving. Stationery = writing materials.
loose / loseLoose = not tight. Lose = fail to keep or misplace.
then / thanThen = time or sequence. Than = comparison.
its / it'sIts = possessive. It's = it is/it has.
your / you'reYour = possessive. You're = you are.
their / there / they'reTheir = possessive; there = place/existence; they're = they are.

Spelling patterns and memory tricks

Pattern/trickExamples
“i before e” is not universal, but works in many common words.believe, achieve, piece
After a short vowel in a stressed syllable, final consonant is often doubled before -ing/-ed.begin → beginning; admit → admitted
Final silent e is often dropped before a vowel suffix.make → making; use → usable
Change y to i before many suffixes when y follows a consonant.happy → happiness; carry → carried
Keep y before -ing.study → studying; carry → carrying
-tion often follows words ending in -te/-t or related forms.educate → education; inform → information
Common -able/-ible words must often be memorized.reliable, available, possible, responsible

Frequently misspelled words

Necessary
One collar, two sleeves: one c, two s.
Separate
There is “a rat” in separate.
Definitely
It contains “finite,” not “definately.”
Accommodation
Two c's and two m's.
Occurrence
Double c, double r.
Embarrass
Double r, double s.
Privilege
No “d” before the final “ge.”
Maintenance
Remember “main,” not “maintainance.”
Roman Urdu trick: Spelling ko sirf dekh kar ratta na lagayen. Word ko syllables mein divide karein, confusing part highlight karein, phir us word ko aik meaningful sentence mein use karein. Context memory ko mazboot karta hai.

Advanced Practice MCQs

Attempt each question, then reveal the answer and explanation.

0 of 20 answered correctly

1. Choose the best edited version: “Due to the fact that the data was incomplete, therefore the conclusion was weak.”

B. It removes redundancy and uses the plural form “data” in formal usage.

2. Which is a sentence fragment?

C. “Although” introduces a dependent clause that cannot stand alone.

3. Select the correctly repaired comma splice.

A. A semicolon correctly joins two closely related independent clauses.

4. Identify the dangling modifier: “After reviewing the report, the conclusion appeared inaccurate.”

D. A conclusion cannot review a report; the sentence needs a human or suitable agent.

5. Choose the parallel sentence.

B. All three items use the -ing form.

6. Choose the correct verb: “The quality of the answers ___ improving.”

C. The head subject is singular “quality”; “of the answers” is an intervening phrase.

7. Choose the correct sentence.

A. “A number of” means several and takes a plural verb.

8. Choose the correct sentence.

D. The head subject is singular “number.”

9. Choose the correct agreement: “Neither the assistants nor the manager ___ available.”

B. With neither...nor, the verb commonly agrees with the nearer subject, “manager.”

10. “Every candidate, along with two assistants, ___ required to sign.”

C. “Every candidate” is singular; “along with” does not change the subject.

11. Choose the correct sentence.

A. “Effect” is the noun meaning result, and “effect” is singular.

12. Select the correct word: “The principal gave useful ___ to the students.”

D. “Advice” is the uncountable noun; “advise” is the verb.

13. Choose the correctly spelled word.

B. “Definitely” contains “finite.”

14. Which spelling is correct?

C. “Accommodation” has two c’s and two m’s.

15. Choose the correct pronoun: “The teacher spoke to Ali and ___.”

A. The pronoun is the object of the preposition “to,” so use “me.”

16. Choose the correct adverb: “The candidate answered the question ___.”

D. “Accurately” modifies the verb “answered.”

17. Choose the correctly edited sentence.

B. It avoids a redundant pronoun and uses a clear reduced modifier.

18. Which sentence is structurally faulty?

C. It is a run-on sentence: two independent clauses lack proper joining punctuation or a conjunction.

19. Choose the correct spelling and meaning.

A. Stationery means writing materials; stationary means not moving.

20. Choose the best revision: “The researcher was not only careful but also recorded the data accurately.”

D. “Careful” and “systematic” are parallel adjectives, and the meaning remains clear.

Final Revision Checklist

  • Separate editing from proofreading: improve ideas first, then mechanics.
  • Check every sentence for a subject, finite verb and complete thought.
  • Repair fragments, run-ons and comma splices.
  • Place modifiers next to the words they logically modify.
  • Keep lists and paired structures parallel.
  • Find the true subject before choosing the verb.
  • Do not let “of,” “along with,” “as well as” or relative clauses distract from agreement.
  • Memorize the difference between “a number of” and “the number of.”
  • Distinguish noun, verb, adjective and adverb functions.
  • Learn confusing pairs through meaning and example sentences.
  • Use spelling patterns as guides, but memorize exceptions.
Master formula: Meaning → structure → agreement → word function → spelling → punctuation. Isi order mein check karne se errors systematically catch honge, warna proofreading aksar “bas commas dekh lena” ban kar reh jati hai.
